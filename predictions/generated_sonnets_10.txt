Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
Attending services with sad eyes,
Yet in black ink their painted faces
Hath been converted from the thing they had before.
Then, were not thy tears as rich as thy praise
Above the din of brass,
Thy fingers thine own deep-sunken eyes were thy enemies.
Were not thy beams the sickle hour,
Which, compounded of greater lengths, would death mine eye send.
  Yet thence I learn,

For that deep wound it gives my friend and me.
Is 't not enough to torture me alone,
That heretic thou thy love may use
To debar me of thee,

So small that my heart cannot bear?
If thou send'st from me thy gentle spirit,
My deepest grief shall be my gain,

9
In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
And my bad, thy good is termed a higer crime.
Thou dost love the bounteous gift of thy lusty days,
That in them thy baddies, if not our great deserts are shown.
As on the finger of a throned queen
The basest jewel will be well esteemed,
Since shames and beauties on thy brow are worse than weeds.
So thou hast passed by the ambush of young days,
Either not assailed, or victor being charged;
  And that in thee my love may be better used
  Than any other's spoilage.


10
Love is my sin, and thy dear virtue hate,
Hate of my sin, grounded on sinful loving.
O, but with mine compare thou thine own state,
Against thy reasons making no defense;
That sin hath in my heart another heart a plea,
One on thy part, another on thy right.
For why have mine eyes out of their spheres been fitted
In thy self-love's fury, to stop posterity?
Thou art thy mother's glass, and she in thee
Calls back the lovely April of her prime;
So thou through windows of thine age shalt see,
Despite thy reasons making no defense.
  And yet, if thou survive thee, I engraft thee a son
  And give him life thine own.

Lo, as a careful huswife runs to catch
One of her feathered creatures broke away,
Sets down her babe, and makes all swift dispatch.
'Tis better to be vile than vile esteemed
When a decrepit husband doth live,
Which tyrannous tyranny doth steal his beauty;
And, for a woman wert thou first created,
Tis better to be vile than despised
When thy husband doth live?
Be thou the tenth wife, ten times more in worth
