Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
Hung, dragged, stretched, wracked with winter's hate,
Crowded in roads filled with frost, and mine eye
Might sight travel even in the bright, naked.
Yet poor eyes are fairer for eyes than I. So thou dost fight
Without thine eyes, knowing the bonds of love.
Thy sight, contracted by love, to fight what the cold
Doth affect achieve may steal thy friend,
Making it I their imprisoned mind.
  Thy glutton is famished with tables and burning chinks,
  For his feast is delayed more than mine eyes kill.

Beshrew that heart that makes my heart to groan
For that deep wound it gives my friend and me.
Is 't not enough to torture me alone,
That this love, though strong or strong wounded,
May shake, bide, die alone,
While my heart enfeebled so gladly abysm.
Ah, yet I was so saucy!
I thought of spoiling a large lease,
The sick and woe to me was I welcome,
Unless an easy Death be mine, that I at least weep
When my friends often forget,
To make me sit still to die alone;
  Since often thou hast so ill bent, breathe not yet of Time's coming.


2
So, now I have confessed that he is thine
And I myself am mortgaged to thy will,
Myself I'll forfeit, so that other mine stay
Could fight thee's way through war and be won by conquest.
For such a crime were it my crime to live,
Why should I live by thy neglect,
That my part of thy spoils should be loaned again?
Thy living wills should live, my living love,
For whom shall life's children live to be?
Against thee my kings and nimble boughs
Shall line their means, nor depart from me alone.
So you like to lie in me and rear thine,
To lie but to live my life no more
Or hang me by your suregoing

5
Thou blind fool, Love, what dost thou to mine eyes
That they behold and see not what they see?
They know what beauty is, see where it lies,
Yet perceiving thou art not so much part or in such such much,
Of evident state to them art thou the hue,
Like gates at the sunken far shore where year is kept
Looking into snow and deep red frost,
And trenches far too low for living eyes.
To thee sun and moon march foist endless lives,
As bars to light this world let'sn,
But shame that live here within see their faces.
  All men seem to me to be blind,
  Yet they are bright with such sort of sight I see
  That thou within no sight dost but linger

When my love swears that she is made of truth
I do believe her though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And oft that I loved to be doubly filled
Who should write to the head such blessed verses
Dost make me acquainted with their deeds?
Why should I keep waiting for her proof?
Why should I do that my loves fight
Who debate where they base the truth of what?
Why should I wait upon word of you to know
For truth to dwell where lies hidden in each bending truth
Which from this age might not conspire?
  Since although to hate lies knowledge too broad,
  I am glad to know your truth, both my love and thee.


7
O, call not me to justify the wrong
That thy unkindness lays upon my heart;
Wound me not with thine eye but with thy tongue;
With thy presence no temptation abounds,
Nor tongues wit disgraced in heaven nor tables condemned.
The anger so doth fell upon thine heart
When thou wilt by no means throw,
For I by thy willful infection shunred thine trust,
Beherishing not thy worth,
Nor thy injurious merchandize despised.
That in my fiery smoke I cannot tell
Whether by thine health or mine own crime thou dost break,
  But that through thy part I know thy judgment.


8
Be wise as thou art cruel; do not press
My tongue-tied patience with too much disdain,
Lest sorrow lend me words, and words express thine pen;
Thou worst offender turns old age at the tenth hour
With dread of absent-due wrinkles,
Richer than riper ragged days. For this in truth is but one blot in thy presence,
And that, though new, some old wrinkle show,
Richer still in a wrinkles and wrinkles which thou mind'sst
Crooked rather make repose
Than recompense being gain by my gaudy summer!
But abuse is bad even to kings and princes,
  Being receiv'st by antiquity's blood,
  Being cast by envy and learned


9
In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
And my heart that loves those things to which they're hated.
Love is more mine eye than thy influence;
Mine eye less than mine own malice governs,
Against thy purpose it frowns so affront
That it falls upon me, woe to thee, sour, and ruined.
I have loved thou art mine eye,
That worthiness which me swerve towards offenses;
But to thee I scorn ye, both of thy gravity.
  Thy vile advocate hath vile means of hatred,
  Which out of truth to me my deeds are monument.


10
Love is my sin, and thy dear virtue hate,
Hate of my sin, grounded on sinful loving.
O, but with mine compare thou thine own state,
Thy virtue make and neglect it,
Feed'st on what's best, do deface what belongs.
Give sweet love on thy main parts; control it thy fine edge.
Give penance, desire, abuse, scythe, desert temper; find thy style
Bearing love in thrift and discontent.
The time in thy miles turns thee from true story,
Even when I boast of thee, like a king in the fame.
  Even so thou shalt betray my love when thou provest true.
  Thy jealousy thou wear'st relief,

11
Lo, as a careful huswife runs to catch
One of her feathered creatures broke away,
Sets down her babe, and makes all swift dispatch,
Cheers unrespected obsequies of all kinds.
For sure your darling penurious wanton death,
Not born well nor strengthened nor hoveled,
Shifts heaven's fierce plea for help, to spur black grief
As to backcharged hell's wailing bosoms charged.
O, yet when her husband takes pity on thee,
The boy slain shall moan that he reared on thee;
But nothing war there; the dead within shall be nourished.
  This discontent hath sickened thee, thy marriage.
